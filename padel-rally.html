Behind the numbers

Useful analytics start
with honest inputs.

This is a small experiment in turning movement into a readable data story, not a certified performance measurement system.

One row means one moment, four positions.

This excerpt uses 31 manually estimated position samples spread across the clip. Court calibration maps the marked floor positions onto a 10 × 20 m plane. The overlays follow those samples, not an AI detector. Jumping players, motion blur, lens distortion and imprecise marks introduce error.

Distance is an estimate, not ground truth.

We sum straight-line distances between keyframes and interpolate between them. Sparse points miss detours; inconsistent marks add jitter. There is no automatic smoothing or AI correction. The figures describe this 15-second excerpt only, not the full point or match, and have not been independently validated.

A heatmap of time, not annotation density.

Interpolated positions are sampled every 0.25 seconds into 1 m cells, weighted by elapsed time. Each player's brightness is scaled to their own busiest cell. This shows estimated occupancy, not a probability or a cross-player intensity comparison. Trails and heatmaps accumulate up to the playhead.

Net time and sample coverage.

Near-net time is the portion of each interpolated movement segment within 3 m of the net. Match totals add the non-overlapping excerpts, keeping player identities consistent; time between excerpts contributes neither distance nor occupancy. A player can have zero time in this strictly defined zone. Key-moment labels are editorial observations, not automatically detected shots.

The footage and its limits.

Only the three selected excerpts are hosted here, with their original broadcast branding intact. The full source video is not distributed. No step counts, ball tracking, shot classification or official player statistics are claimed.

Each curated excerpt can be 1–60 seconds long. The catalog in assets/padel-rally-data.js records absolute source start times, excerpt durations, published clip paths, complete position samples, court calibration and key moments. Playback starts at zero in each trimmed clip; source timestamps still refer to the original recording. Keep excerpts chronological and non-overlapping. Mark the entire new excerpt; do not stretch the existing paths to fit. Use a continuous 16:9 camera view without cuts or zooms within each excerpt. Adding footage requires permission to publish it.
